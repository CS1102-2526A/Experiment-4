Simulation - Determining the pH of a Weak-Acid Strong-Base Solution

In this experiment, we will continue use red cabbage water as our pH indicator.

Materials we needed:

  • Red Cabbage Water
  • Black Coffee
  • Soapy Water

Step 1: Add black coffee (pH 5) to the red cabbage water. The colour will turn pink because the black coffee is an acidic liquid.

Step 2: Add the same amount of soapy water (pH 11) to the same solution. You will observe that the colour changes rapidly. It flashes green for a second, then turns purple-blue, and finally becomes blue.


In Step 2, the red cabbage water turns green because soapy water is a strong alkaline liquid. When red cabbage water meets with an alkaline liquid, its colour changes from purple-blue to green. Furthermore, since the red cabbage water has already been added to the black coffee, the combination of an acidic liquid with an alkaline liquid causes neutralization.

This reaction causes the red cabbage water to turn purple-blue for a second. Finally, because soapy water is a strong alkaline and black coffee is a weak acid, the final mixture becomes a slightly alkaline liquid (~pH 8 - 10). Therefore, the red cabbage water turns blue in the end.
